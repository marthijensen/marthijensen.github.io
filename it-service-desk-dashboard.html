The business problem

Turn ticket data into a usable service-management view.

IT service leaders need to understand more than the number of tickets coming in. They need a clear view of backlog, issue patterns, response speed, resolution performance, and how work is distributed across support teams. This project translates a large simulated ticket dataset into a dashboard experience designed for operational decision-making.

Questions answered

How many tickets are created, resolved, or still open? Which issue themes and channels generate demand? Are urgent tickets receiving faster service?

Data used

A synthetic IT service desk dataset with ticket status, priority, issue topic, support channel, agent group, timestamps, support level, and technician information.

Key findings

What the dashboard revealed.

Stable weekly demand

Weekly ticket volume remained relatively stable at roughly 5,100–5,400 tickets, creating a predictable baseline for staffing and support-capacity planning.

Backlog visibility matters

Open and in-progress tickets represented the largest active workload, making ticket status and backlog monitoring important for operational prioritization.

Demand is broadly distributed

Issue topics, support teams, and ticket channels were closely balanced. No single category dominated volume, so improvement opportunities should be monitored across the whole service desk.

Priority affects service speed

Critical tickets received the fastest first response and resolution, while lower-priority requests took longer, reflecting a logical urgency-based service pattern.
